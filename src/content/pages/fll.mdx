# Starting an FLL Team

<Alert title="FIRST LEGO League ends after this season" variant="warning">
  FIRST and LEGO Education are ending their partnership, so BIOGLOW is the final FLL season. Next year the two organizations run separate competitions: FIRST is building its own K-8 robotics programs, and LEGO Education is launching a new LEGO League in August 2027.

  Both are still being designed, and the details are thin, but a similar program will be there to work toward. Everything below still carries over: a couple adults, a kit, a table, and a few hours a week. If you are thinking about a team, we can help either way.
</Alert>

FIRST LEGO League (FLL) is where a lot of our members started. Students in roughly grades 2 through 8 build a LEGO robot, program it through a season of missions, research a real-world problem, and explain both to judges. The season runs from the August kickoff to qualifiers in December and January.

## Explore or Challenge

* **Explore** (grades 2 to 4) is the entry point. Up to six students build a model and a simple robot with SPIKE Essential, then show their work at a festival-style event with no rankings or eliminations.
* **Challenge** (grades 4 to 8) is the competition. Up to ten students build and program a robot for 2.5-minute matches, complete an Innovation Project, and meet judges who score them on Core Values as much as on the robot.

Ages vary by region, and this season FIRST is running two editions side by side: Founders Edition on SPIKE, and Future Edition on the newer Computer Science & AI kits. Ask FIRST Florida which one your local events use before you buy hardware.

LEGO Education stopped selling SPIKE Prime and SPIKE Essential on June 30, 2026. Both kits stay legal through the 2027-28 season, and older EV3, NXT, and Robot Inventor sets are still allowed, so a used kit, a school loaner, or a set already in a closet is enough.

## Inside a Challenge Season

Challenge has four equally weighted parts: Core Values, Robot Design, the Innovation Project, and the Robot Game. A qualifier scores all four, so a team can advance on its judging as well as its matches.

### The Robot Game

Every team plays the same game on the same field. The 2026-27 game is called BIOGLOW, and it has 15 missions built around biodiversity. In each 2.5-minute match the robot runs on its own code with no remote control, scoring whatever missions the team chooses in whatever order they choose.

This year the missions include flying a drone on a survey, spreading exploding seeds, flipping a rock, sorting leaves in a nest without letting the katydid escape, and installing upgrades such as a nesting canopy or a garden skylight on a dock. Touching the robot outside the launch area costs a precision token, and tokens are worth points. Teams play three matches at a qualifier and keep their best score.

### The Innovation Project

Teams find a problem that puts biodiversity at risk, research it, and design a solution. Judges look for the engineering design process: what the problem is, who it affects, what already exists, what the team made or prototyped, and what testing taught them. The students present for about five minutes, then answer questions. Adults can help prepare, but the students give the presentation.

### Robot Design

A separate session where students explain the robot and the code to judges: why the drive base is shaped the way it is, what each attachment does, how the code decides where to drive, and what they tried that did not work. Judges score the process, not just the result.

## The Robot and the Table

A SPIKE Prime set is enough for a Challenge robot. It has a hub with six ports, two large motors, one medium motor, and color, distance, and force sensors, plus a few hundred Technic pieces.

Most rookie robots follow the same pattern: two driven wheels, a caster or a pair of omni wheels, and a frame small enough to start inside the launch area. Teams build two to four quick-change attachments for their highest-value missions, then spend the rest of the season making those runs reliable. Code is written in Word Blocks or Python in the SPIKE app. In our experience, repeatable driving beats clever mechanisms, and a low battery changes how far the robot rolls, so charge before every practice match.

The game is played on a mat about four feet by eight feet with low walls, which is the one piece of equipment worth building early. Teams can practice on a half field if space is tight.

## What It Costs

* FIRST registration is $285 for a team of up to ten, and the BIOGLOW Challenge Set, which has the mat, mission models, and rulebook, is $105 more plus shipping.
* A SPIKE Prime kit ran about $400 to $550 new before it was discontinued. Used kits and school loaners are the usual route for a new team now.
* Qualifiers charge event fees locally, often $50 to $150 per team.
* Shirts, snacks, travel, and lumber for a table make up the rest.

A first Challenge season usually lands between $800 and $1,200 if the team buys a robot, and under $500 if it borrows one. Explore registration is $150 and includes the Explore set, and Explore teams meet less often.

## The Weekly Schedule

Plan on two meetings of about 3.5 hours each week. That is enough to build the field, teach the robot, run the project, and still leave time for a practice match. Around mid-November, as qualifiers get close, most teams add a session or push into weekends for driving practice and judging rehearsals.

The last two weeks are not for new mechanisms. They are for making the robot consistent and for practicing the presentation out loud.

## Managing a Team That Lasts

* Split the work. One adult for the robot and one for forms, money, and travel. A parent coordinator buys the coach a season.
* Give the students the jobs. Judges ask students what they built and why. A robot a parent built reads as a parent-built robot.
* Keep one budget: registration, game set, kit, event fees, shirts, and travel.
* Recruit a grade younger than you need. FLL teams fall apart when their oldest students age out of Challenge.
* Write down what worked. One page at the end of the season is the difference between a rebuild and a repeat.
